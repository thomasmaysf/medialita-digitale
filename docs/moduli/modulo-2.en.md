# Module 2 — Symbols, phone materials, and photography

**Theme:** how symbols and images carry emotion over time; what a smartphone is made of and where those materials come from; how photography moved from film to digital files — and why **pixels** and **colour** connect to the binary ideas in [Module 1](modulo-1.en.md).

!!! info "Our edition (Trento 2026/27)"
    - **Date:** Saturday 14 November 2026
    - **Local facilitators:** Camilla (Part A), Consuelo / Elisa / Mpaliza (Part B), Thomas / Stefano (Part C); optional **Part D** evening with Consuelo / Elisa / Mpaliza
    - **Venue:** TBD

**Why it matters:** Linking art, geography, and technology to objects young people use every day helps them read emoji and photos more critically, ask *where* materials come from and *who* pays the cost of production, and see that every image on a screen is also a grid of numbers.

---

## Learning goals

By the end of the module, young people and parents should be able to:

- See that **symbols** (cave art, hieroglyphs, icons, emoji) carry shared meaning, while **different cultures** and **generations** may read them differently.
- Invent and **decode** a small symbol system in pairs or groups, explaining rules and ambiguity.
- Name **key materials** in a smartphone (cobalt, lithium, tantalum/coltan, rare earths, gold, copper, glass, plastics…) and link them **roughly** to world regions, without stereotyping people or places.
- Describe, in age-appropriate language, **impacts** of mining and manufacturing (environment, health, child labour in some supply chains), device **lifespan**, **repair**, and **recycling** — without blaming young people for owning a phone.
- Explain in simple words the chain **light → film / sensor → file**, connecting film and pixels to **analog vs digital** and the **grid** from Module 1.
- Try **RGB**, coloured gels, and (where possible) a webcam or app to change colours and pixels, noticing what changes in the image.

## Part A — From symbols to emoji {#part-a-from-symbols-to-emoji}

*Approximate time: 9:00–10:20 (~80 min).*

### Intent

Show that communicating **without words** is as old as humanity: marks on rock, symbolic writing, religious and commercial icons, up to **emoji** — same need (say something fast), different media and rules.

### Suggested internal timing

| Block | Duration | Approximate time |
|-------|----------|------------------|
| Welcome + timeline presentation | 20 min | 9:00–9:20 |
| “Invent / decode symbols” activity | 35 min | 9:20–9:55 |
| Emoji: cultures and generations | 20 min | 9:55–10:15 |
| Wrap-up and questions | 5 min | 10:15–10:20 |

### Step 1 — Presentation: a timeline of symbols (~20 min)

1. **Welcome (5 min):** who you are, short ground rules (respect, one voice at a time, parents take part). Light callback to Module 1: *“Today we start from how **images** communicate and what’s **inside** the phone.”*
2. **Five stops on the board or slides (15 min):** for each stop, **one image** and **one question**.

| Era / form | Examples (to show) | Key message |
|------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Cave art | Lascaux, Altamira (reproductions) | Marks for hunting, story, ritual — **context** matters |
| Hieroglyphs | Name cartouche, simplified papyrus | **Phonetic** symbols + **ideograms**; few could read |
| Medieval icons / signs | Byzantine icon, cross, signage | **Shared** symbols in a community |
| Modern pictograms | WC, recycling, road signs | International standards; rare ambiguity |
| Emoji | Chat with 😀🎉📱 | Speed, emotional tone; **Unicode** standard |

!!! abstract "Key idea"
    A symbol does not “mean” alone: it means when **readers** share rules and context.

**Facilitation:** avoid long date lists; prefer **visual contrast** (same emotion — fear, joy — expressed differently).

### Step 2 — Activity: invent and decode symbols (~35 min)

#### Materials

- A4 sheets folded in half (“official” code / “decode” sheet)
- Pencils, markers; optional card for a “symbol tablet”

#### Procedure

1. **Mixed pairs (5 min):** each pair is **one young person + one parent** (or two young people with a roaming adult).
2. **Invent 5 symbols (12 min):** the pair creates **5 simple drawings** for facilitator-assigned ideas (e.g. *happy*, *tired*, *hungry*, *I want to go*, *stop / enough*). Rule: **max 3 strokes** per symbol, no letters.
3. **Sheet swap (8 min):** two pairs exchange sheets **without** oral explanation. The other pair tries to **decode** by writing under each symbol what they think it means.
4. **Compare (10 min):** pairs reveal original intent. On the board, note **ambiguity** (e.g. star = wish or night?).

!!! tip "Facilitator"
    Walk between tables; celebrate creative solutions. If a pair finishes early, ask for a **6th secret symbol** for a neighbour only.

#### Adaptations

| Group | Adaptation |
|-------|------------|
| **Younger (grades 4–5)** | Only **3 symbols**; concrete ideas (eat, sleep, play). |
| **Older (grades 7–9)** | Add a **combined** symbol (two symbols = phrase), like hieroglyphs. |
| **Multilingual families** | Allow labels in **two languages** under the symbol — discuss **Unicode** as a “world alphabet” for emoji. |

### Step 3 — Emoji: cultures and generations (~20 min)

1. **Project or hand out** the same emoji-only message (e.g. “dinner tonight? 🍕👍” vs “👀🍕❓”).
2. **Hand-raise poll:** *“Who gets it instantly? Who prefers words?”*
3. **Three comparison axes (guided discussion):**

| Axis | Questions |
|------|-----------|
| **Generations** | Do parents use the same emoji as young people? What gets misread (e.g. 👍 vs 😂)? |
| **Cultures** | Same emoji, different meaning? (Teaching examples: gestures that differ — without mocking.) |
| **Platforms** | Same emoji, different look on Apple / Google / Samsung — why that confuses. |

4. **Mini task (5 min):** each pair rewrites **one sentence** words-only, then emoji-only, then **mixed** — which version carries **tone** best?

### Circle questions (Part A)

1. When is a symbol “clear” and when is it not?
2. Are emoji a **language** or a **tool**?
3. Have you ever misread a message because of an emoji or icon?
4. If we drew a symbol for “respect for the planet”, what would it look like?

### Part A wrap-up

Recap: **symbol + context + reader**. Tease Part B: *“The phone also has **symbols** on screen — and **materials** from around the world.”*

## Part B — What’s inside a smartphone {#part-b-whats-inside-a-smartphone}

*Approximate time: 10:40–12:00 (~80 min).*

### Intent

Help participants see a smartphone not only as screen and apps, but as an **assembly of materials** with **geographies** and **consequences** — environment, health, labour — and how **lifespan**, **repair**, and **recycling** choices matter.

### Suggested internal timing

| Block | Duration | Approximate time |
|-------|----------|------------------|
| Intro + key materials | 25 min | 10:40–11:05 |
| “Trace the materials” lab | 40 min | 11:05–11:45 |
| Repair, recycling, wrap-up | 15 min | 11:45–12:00 |

!!! warning "Sensitive content (required)"
    In some supply chains (especially **cobalt** and **coltan** in parts of **Central Africa**, including the **DR Congo**) working conditions are dangerous and **child labour** exists. Treat this **without violent images**, **without blaming** young people for phones in their pockets, and offer **opt-out** (quiet space, alternative task with a blank map). One adult stays available for anyone who steps out.

### Step 1 — Introduction: materials and regions (~25 min)

#### Typical materials and roles (simplified)

| Material | Where it is often sourced / processed (simplified) | Role in the phone |
|----------|-----------------------------------------------------|-------------------|
| **Cobalt** | DR Congo, other regions | Battery (lithium-ion) |
| **Lithium** | Chile, Australia, Argentina, … | Battery |
| **Tantalum** (coltan) | DR Congo, Australia, Brazil, … | Capacitors, miniaturized electronics |
| **Rare earths** | China, USA, Australia, … | Magnets, display colours, vibration |
| **Gold** | Various regions (Africa, South America, …) | Electrical contacts (small amounts) |
| **Copper** | Global | Wiring, circuits |
| **Glass / silicon** | Global | Screen, chips (silicon) |
| **Plastic / aluminium** | Often manufactured in Asia | Body, structure |

!!! important "Geographic precision"
    Maps are **pedagogical**, not day-by-day mining reports. Say clearly: *“Mines change; what matters is the **idea** of a global chain and responsibility.”*

#### Impacts (age-appropriate language)

- **Environment:** deforestation, water and soil pollution, energy for extraction and smelting.
- **People:** workers’ health, dangerous conditions, resource-related conflict in some historical zones.
- **Child labour:** in **some** artisanal cobalt mines, children and teens work in unsafe conditions — documented by groups such as **Amnesty International** (see links below). We are not “bad” for having a phone; these are **systems** to understand and **pressure** (laws, conscious buying, recycling) we can apply.

**Links for facilitators and parents:**

- [Amnesty International — Child labour behind smart phone batteries (2016)](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2016/01/child-labour-behind-smart-phone-batteries/)
- [UNEP — e-waste / electronic waste](https://www.unep.org/) (search “e-waste” for updated reports)
- [Fairphone — material reports](https://www.fairphone.com/) (material and supply-chain transparency)

### Step 2 — Lab: “Trace the materials” (~40 min)

#### Materials

- World map (floor, table, or wall)
- One card per material (name, icon, short note)
- Coloured string or yarn, washi tape, pins (if vertical map)
- Optional: phone opened **externally only** / parts diagram

#### Step-by-step

1. **Map centre (5 min):** place a **smartphone silhouette** in the middle (print or drawing). Read aloud: *“Every phone is a puzzle of worlds.”*
2. **Groups (3–4 groups, 5 min setup):** each group gets **2–3 material cards** and one string colour.
3. **Quick research (10 min):** with the facilitator’s guide, groups read the card and **attach** string from the card toward the **region** shown (kilometre precision not needed). One adult per group prevents gross errors.
4. **Gallery walk (10 min):** each group explains **one** chain in 30 seconds.
5. **Overlaps (10 min):** on the board, list regions with **many strings** — *“Why do so many materials pass through a few countries? What does **dependence** mean?”*
6. **Personal reflection (5 min):** *“One thing you didn’t know and one question you take home.”* — in notebooks, sharing optional.

!!! tip "Geography / materials facilitator"
    Keep a **calm** tone; validate feelings (“it’s normal to feel overwhelmed”). Connect to the **right to know** and **recycling** as possible actions.

### Step 3 — Lifespan, repair, recycling (~15 min)

| Topic | Points to cover |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Useful life** | Often a few years; software updates that slow devices; pressure to upgrade |
| **Repair** | Screen, battery — **right to repair** debated in the EU; ask who has repaired something |
| **Recycling** | e-waste: metal recovery vs landfill; municipal collection; **do not** bin electronics at home |

**Key message:** stay informed, use devices longer, repair when you can, recycle properly, and (for adults) consider products with transparent **reports**.

### Group questions (Part B)

1. Which material surprised you most?
2. Why does such a small object need the **whole world**?
3. What can **families**, **schools**, **companies**, **governments** do — one action each?
4. Is it right for a child to work in a mine for our phone? What should change?

### Adaptations

| Group | Adaptation |
|-------|------------|
| **Younger** | Only **4 materials** and strings; short stories, no dense statistics |
| **Older** | Add **value chain** (extraction → refining → factory → shop); homework: find local **WEEE** recycling label |
| **Opt-out** | Alternative: colour a blank map with invented “fantasy materials” + 1:1 chat |

## Part C — Photography: from film to file {#part-c-photography-from-film-to-file}

*Approximate time: 12:00–13:20 (~80 min).*

### Intent

Connect **camera obscura**, **film / slides**, **digital sensor**, and **files**; show that a digital image is **numbers** (pixels, RGB) — same thread as [Module 1](modulo-1.en.md) (binary, coarse vs fine grid, analog wave).

### Suggested internal timing

| Block | Duration | Approximate time |
|-------|----------|------------------|
| Quick history + pixel numbers | 25 min | 12:00–12:25 |
| RGB and colour gels | 25 min | 12:25–12:50 |
| Mini camera obscura / pinhole | 25 min | 12:50–13:15 |
| Wrap-up and badge stamp | 5 min | 13:15–13:20 |

### Step 1 — From light to numbers (~25 min)

1. **Camera obscura (5 min):** hole in a box → **upside-down** image on the back. *“Light **draws** without electricity.”*
2. **Film and slides (8 min):** chemistry that **captures** light; projected positive = ancestor of cinema and stories (metaphor).
3. **Digital sensor (7 min):** grid of **photodiodes** → electrical signal → **file**. Explicit link to Module 1:

| Module 1 | Module 2 (photo) |
|----------|------------------|
| Analog wave vs sampling | Continuous light vs **pixels** |
| Coarse / fine grid | Resolution (MP = millions of pixels) |
| Bits 0/1 | Values per colour channel (e.g. 0–255 for R, G, B) |

4. **Orders of magnitude (5 min):** 12 MP ≈ 12 million dots; Full HD screen ≈ 2 MP; *“More pixels ≠ always a better photo”* (lens, light, eye).

!!! abstract "Key idea"
    Film and sensor **record light**; digital **measures** and **stores measurements** — like bits measuring on/off states.

### Step 2 — RGB, gels, and webcam lab (~25 min)

#### Materials

- Three **R, G, B** gels (or red/green/blue cellophane)
- Torch or white light
- Laptop with **webcam** + simple app (OS camera, or “pixelate” / colour-adjust app — test beforehand)

#### Procedure

1. **Additivity (10 min):** overlap R+G+B on light → approach **white**; remove one colour → perceived tint.
2. **Screen (5 min):** zoom a photo: see **stairsteps**? Link to **pixels**.
3. **Stations (10 min):** in rotation, young people try a **colour filter** on webcam or RGB sliders in an app; parent notes a sketch or short description in the notebook.

!!! tip "Photography / tech facilitator"
    Prepare **2–3 laptops** to avoid queues; one adult per station. Disable cloud accounts for privacy.

### Step 3 — Mini pinhole / camera obscura activity (~25 min)

#### Materials (per group)

- Shoebox or ~20×30 cm cardboard
- 5×5 cm aluminium foil, fine needle
- Opaque tape (black electrical tape)
- Matte photo paper or white A5 (inside back of box)
- Scissors (adults only for cutting)

#### Build (guided)

1. **Adults** cut a 3×3 cm window on one short side; tape foil with **pinhole** (~1 mm) in the centre.
2. Inside back: glued **white** paper (screen).
3. **Lid** sealed with tape; room as **dark as possible** near the box.
4. Point pinhole toward a **bright window** (not direct sun into eyes); observe **faint upside-down** image — move the box slowly.

**Safety:** no direct sun through the hole toward eyes; needles adults only.

#### Adaptations

| Group | Adaptation |
|-------|------------|
| **Younger** | One facilitator-built box demo; draw “what I see” |
| **Older** | Rough **magnification** math; link to exposure time and ISO |
| **Short on time** | Skip build; demo + light-path diagram only |

### Circle questions (Part C)

1. What do film and sensor have in common?
2. Why does the computer need **numbers** for colours?
3. If each pixel were one bit (black/white), how many bits for a small photo?
4. Do you prefer photos on paper or on a phone — why?

### Morning wrap-up

Recap **three threads**: symbols, materials, light→numbers. **Badge stamp** if your programme uses one. Soft invite to optional Part D evening.

## Part D (optional) — Parents’ evening {#part-d-parents-evening}

*Time and venue: TBD (~90 min).*

### Intent

Space **mainly for adults** (young people optional if desired) to go deeper on **human and environmental cost** of devices, **family agreements** about phones, and concrete actions — without a preachy tone.

### Suggested outline

| Min | Content |
|-----|---------|
| 0–10 | Welcome, evening goals, respect ground rules |
| 10–35 | Slides summarizing Part B: chains, child labour (report reference), e-waste |
| 35–55 | Table discussion: “What do we know? What can we change?” |
| 55–75 | Family digital agreements: screen time, overnight charging, repair vs upgrade |
| 75–90 | Q&A, resources (Fairphone, local WEEE, suggested documentaries with disclaimer) |

### Discussion prompts

1. How **transparent** is the supply chain of the phone we use?
2. **Reuse** in the family: handing phones down to siblings — pros/cons?
3. How do we talk to young people about **child labour** without trauma or denial?
4. One **action** within a month (recycling, repair, letter to a manufacturer, house rules).

!!! tip "Evening facilitation"
    Let parents speak; avoid single “right” answers. Capture **3 ideas** to share in the wiki for the next edition.
